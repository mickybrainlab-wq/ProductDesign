This is one of the deepest questions in psychology.

There aren't exactly "7" or "12" resistance types in the brain. Different researchers classify them differently. But if we combine neuroscience, psychology, behavioral economics, evolutionary biology, and cognitive science, almost every resistance falls into **8 fundamental categories**.

The goal should not be **0 resistance**. Some resistance (like avoiding genuine danger) is adaptive and keeps you alive. The goal is **0 unnecessary resistance** to actions that align with your long-term goals.

---

# The 8 Fundamental Resistances

```text
                 ACTION
                    ▲
                    │
      ┌─────────────┼─────────────┐
      │             │             │
 Energy        Fear/Risk      Uncertainty
      │             │             │
 Reward       Skill Gap       Overload
      │             │             │
 Habit         Identity      Environment
```

Every time you procrastinate, at least one of these systems is creating friction.

---

# 1. Energy Resistance

Your brain asks:

> "Can I afford this?"

This system evolved because energy was scarce.

Symptoms:

* "I'll do it later."
* Feeling mentally heavy before starting.
* Wanting to lie down or scroll instead.

### Protocol

Lower activation energy.

Instead of:

> Study Graphs

Do:

```text
Open VS Code

↓

Open one problem

↓

Read one line

↓

Draw one graph
```

Once you're moving, the brain often updates its estimate that the task is manageable.

---

# 2. Fear Resistance

Your brain asks:

> "Will this hurt me?"

Today that might mean failure, embarrassment, rejection, or uncertainty rather than physical danger.

Symptoms:

* Perfectionism
* Avoiding interviews
* Never shipping projects

### Protocol

Shrink the perceived threat.

Instead of:

> Build a startup.

Do:

> Build one screen.

The threat decreases dramatically.

---

# 3. Uncertainty Resistance

Your brain asks:

> "What exactly am I supposed to do?"

The brain dislikes ambiguity.

Symptoms:

* Feeling stuck before beginning.
* Endless planning.

### Protocol

Always define the next visible action.

Instead of:

> Learn Graphs

Write:

```text
Draw graph

↓

Find pattern

↓

Trace BFS

↓

Code BFS
```

Clarity reduces friction.

---

# 4. Reward Resistance

Your brain asks:

> "Why should I spend effort?"

If the reward is months away, motivation is weak.

Symptoms:

* Social media feels more attractive.
* Learning feels "dry."

### Protocol

Create immediate feedback.

For example:

```text
Learn DFS

↓

Animate DFS

↓

Show a traversal

↓

Feel progress
```

Visible progress is more rewarding than invisible knowledge.

---

# 5. Skill Gap Resistance

Your brain asks:

> "Can I actually do this?"

If the gap feels too large, the brain predicts failure.

Symptoms:

* "I'm not ready."

### Protocol

Reduce difficulty by one level.

If Medium feels impossible,

solve Easy.

If Easy feels impossible,

trace someone else's solution.

---

# 6. Habit Resistance

Your brain asks:

> "Is this what we usually do?"

Habits reduce cognitive effort.

Symptoms:

* Automatically opening YouTube.
* Picking up your phone without thinking.

### Protocol

Don't fight the habit directly.

Replace it.

Instead of:

```text
Open Phone
```

Train:

```text
Open LeetCode
```

Keep the cue but change the routine.

---

# 7. Identity Resistance

Your brain asks:

> "Is this who I am?"

People often behave consistently with their self-image.

Symptoms:

* "I'm not creative."
* "I'm bad at math."

### Protocol

Collect evidence that contradicts the limiting identity.

Instead of saying:

> I am a programmer.

Build small programs regularly.

Repeated evidence reshapes identity more effectively than repeating affirmations.

---

# 8. Environmental Resistance

Your brain asks:

> "What does this environment encourage?"

Humans respond strongly to cues around them.

Symptoms:

* Studying poorly in a room full of distractions.
* Reaching for a phone that's always visible.

### Protocol

Redesign the environment.

Examples:

* Put the phone in another room.
* Keep your IDE open.
* Leave your notebook on the desk.
* Reduce the number of decisions needed to begin.

---

# The Master Equation

You can think of resistance like this:

```text
Resistance =
Energy Cost
+ Fear
+ Uncertainty
+ Delay to Reward
+ Skill Gap
+ Habit Conflict
+ Identity Conflict
+ Environmental Friction
```

If any one of these becomes large, starting becomes harder.

---

# The Creator's Protocol

If your goal is to build products, companies, or solve difficult engineering problems, ask these eight questions before every session:

1. Do I have enough energy?
2. What am I afraid of?
3. Is the next step crystal clear?
4. What immediate feedback will I get?
5. Is this difficulty appropriate?
6. What habit am I replacing?
7. What identity am I reinforcing?
8. Does my environment help or hurt?

If you can answer all eight well, action becomes much easier.

## One final insight

Notice that none of these resistances are enemies. They are prediction systems.

Your brain is constantly estimating:

* **Energy:** "Can I afford this?"
* **Safety:** "Is this dangerous?"
* **Value:** "Is this worth it?"
* **Competence:** "Can I succeed?"
* **Consistency:** "Is this what I usually do?"

The most effective strategy is not to fight these systems, but to provide evidence that the task is **safe, worthwhile, clear, and achievable**. When those predictions change, resistance naturally decreases and action becomes much easier.
